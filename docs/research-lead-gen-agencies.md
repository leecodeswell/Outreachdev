# How lead-gen and contractor-marketing companies actually work in 2026

Prepared November 5, 2026 for Lee Thompson / Pari. Research from the public websites of the
largest players and a set of smaller contractor-focused agencies, plus industry roundups. Figures are
as published by the companies themselves or by competitor roundups; treat them as directional.

## 1. The landscape: four business models

### A. Shared-lead marketplaces (Angi/HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Networx, Porch, Houzz Pro)
- **Model**: homeowner submits a request; the platform sells it to several pros at once. Thumbtack
  says pros pay per lead with a budget they set and "limited competition per lead"; Angi's pro page
  promises "over 1.5M opportunities last month" and says nothing about price.
- **Money**: pay per lead or per contact, $20 to $300+ depending on trade; no subscription on
  Thumbtack; Angi has historically combined membership plus per-lead fees.
- **Reputation**: Angi Leads has ~37,000 Trustpilot reviews around 1.4/5; the FTC filed a complaint
  against HomeAdvisor in 2022 for misrepresenting lead quality and source. BBB lists thousands of
  complaints. Contractors' consistent objections: the same lead sold 3 to 8 times, unresponsive
  homeowners, credits instead of refunds, the platform owning the profile and reviews.
- **Why it matters for Pari**: the single most effective positioning for a new entrant is to be the
  explicit opposite of this model. Every small competitor studied leads with it.

### B. Pay-per-call / exclusive-call networks (Service Direct, Modernize, CraftJack now folded into Angi)
- **Model**: the network runs ads and publisher sites, routes phone calls to one contractor, charges
  per valid call. Service Direct: contractor sets its own cost per lead, no setup fee, no contract,
  claims 8.5x average ROI and $950M revenue generated since 2006.
- **Money**: $30 to $200+ per call depending on category and area; the network sets the floor.
- **Observations**: these sell "exclusive" hard and sit between marketplaces and agencies. Their
  pages list trades served, a 4-step "how it works", client logos (Terminix, Roto-Rooter), ROI
  stats and a dense FAQ on billable vs non-billable leads.

### C. Exclusive-lead retainers (S&J Business Builders and similar small operators)
- **Model**: in-house callers find and qualify opt-in homeowners in agreed ZIP codes; each lead goes
  to one buyer. S&J hero: "Stop sharing leads. Start closing exclusives." $300 trial for 4 to 7
  leads, $800/week, $3,000 to $12,000/month tiers, SEO add-on $3,500. Replacement, not refund, for
  bad leads. Explicit DNC-compliance language in the FAQ.
- **This is the closest model to what Pari is positioned as.** It is run by two founders, uses a
  founders section as a trust signal, and publishes flat prices.

### D. Full-service contractor marketing agencies (Hook Agency, Builder Funnel, Contractor Dynamics, Roofing Webmasters, Rival Digital, Footbridge Media)
- **Model**: monthly retainers for SEO, websites, Google Ads/LSA, Meta ads, content, with the
  contractor "generating their own exclusive leads" from assets they own.
- **Money (published)**: Hook: Local SEO+AEO $3,750/mo, Google Ads+LSA from $2,000/mo, Meta from
  $3,000/mo, websites $1,000 to $2,000/mo over 12 months. Builder Funnel: up to $7,500/mo
  "Accelerate". Footbridge: $249/mo all-in at the bottom. Roundups put the range at $249 to $7,500
  per month, with most serious agencies at $2,000 to $5,000.
- **Positioning**: Hook: "Most contractors don't have a lead problem. They have a presence problem."
  Builder Funnel: "Builder Funnel isn't a marketing agency" (growth partner), builders and remodelers
  only, "measured by signed projects, revenue and gross margin". Many publish a minimum client size
  ($2M to $3M+ revenue).

## 2. What their websites have in common

Across all four models the page structure is nearly identical. This is the pattern the new site
follows, with the trust-signal section adapted to what Pari can truthfully claim today.

1. **Hero**: outcome headline in the contractor's language ("Find more customers", "Stop sharing
   leads", "Get leads, win jobs"), one primary CTA ("Book an intro call", "Get started", "Apply now"),
   one secondary ("See how it works"). Often a phone number next to the CTA.
2. **Logo strip or stat strip** directly under the hero. New agencies without logos use a stat
   strip of commitments (100% exclusive, no contract) instead.
3. **Problem framing** aimed at marketplaces: shared leads, price shoppers, platform owns the
   relationship. Usually a two-column "without us / with us" comparison.
4. **How it works in 3 or 4 steps**, numbered.
5. **Service pillars**, 3 to 5 cards, written as outcomes not tactics ("Get calls while you sleep").
6. **Trades served** list with links to trade pages (roofing, HVAC, remodeling, etc.). Trade pages
   are the main SEO asset.
7. **Proof**: testimonials with names and companies, case studies with a number, review count,
   awards (Inc. 5000, Google Partner), founder photo and story for the smaller shops.
8. **Pricing**: either published tiers (Hook, S&J) or a lead-qualification form that asks revenue
   band and marketing spend before a call (Builder Funnel, Modernize). Several publish "no setup fee,
   no contract" as the pricing promise.
9. **FAQ**: 6 to 10 questions, almost always including "are leads exclusive", "how much", "is
   there a contract", "how fast", "what makes you different".
10. **Closing CTA band** with the phone number.
11. **Footer**: full postal address, phone, trades links, legal links, social icons. Address and
    phone are also in LocalBusiness schema on the strong sites.

Visual style notes: the big marketplaces are flat, white, blue/green, utilitarian. The agencies
split between loud (Hook: neon accents, caps, emojis) and premium (Builder Funnel: dark, serif
headlines, lots of whitespace). Nobody in the contractor space is doing glass/cream/royal blue; it
will read as more premium than the field, which fits the "best contractor in the market" audience.

## 3. Marketing at each stage of growth

| Stage | Who they are | What they actually do for marketing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zero to first 5 clients** | solo founder | Founder-led cold outreach to a tight niche and geography, referrals from existing relationships, a free audit or preview as the hook, posting experiments on LinkedIn/Facebook, local trade groups. One guide reports startup cost under $2,000 and first revenue in 1 to 2 weeks for a service (done-for-you) model. |
| **5 to 20 clients** | 1 to 3 people | Case studies from the first clients, Google Business Profile and local SEO for the agency itself, "best agencies for X" roundup placement, a lead magnet (pricing guide, lead-cost calculator), webinars or in-person talks at local builder associations. |
| **20 to 100 clients** | small team | Paid search on "roofing marketing agency" type terms, niche podcasts and YouTube, partnerships with suppliers and software (JobNimbus, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro all have partner directories), trade show booths, referral programs. |
| **Marketplaces** | hundreds of staff | Consumer brand advertising to homeowners (TV, display), affiliate publisher networks, financing products, acquisitions (Modernize bought Get The Referral; Angi absorbed CraftJack). |

Common thread at every size: **specialization and proof**. The agencies that publish a minimum
client size and a single vertical charge the most.

## 4. Starting from scratch in 2026: what the sources agree on

1. **Pick one niche and one geography first.** Multi-trade, multi-state comes later. For Pari:
   higher-ticket residential trades, Iowa plus the adjacent Minnesota/Nebraska metros.
2. **Done-for-you service, not rank-and-rent or lead reselling.** Lower startup cost, faster first
   revenue, no inventory of leads to pre-fund.
3. **Lead with a low-risk offer.** Free preview/audit (already Lee's pattern), a trial week, or a
   first-month guarantee. S&J's $300 trial and Thumbtack's "no subscription" exist for this reason.
4. **Get the first clients through conversations, not ads.** Cold email, cold calls and local
   groups, with the research-first personalization Lee already uses.
5. **Write the simplest possible agreement**: price, what a qualified lead is, delivery method,
   replacement policy, how to cancel. One page.
6. **Report weekly** in plain English; this is what keeps month-to-month clients.
7. **Build proof deliberately**: ask for a testimonial and a number at day 30 and day 90, publish
   case studies on the trade pages, request Google reviews on the agency's own profile.
8. **Keep the stack cheap**: Workspace, a free CRM, a verification tool, a warm-up tool, Canva,
   and the website you already own.

## 5. Pricing models compared, for Pari's decision

| Model | Pros | Cons | Fit for Pari now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pay per lead | easy to sell | you pre-fund the marketing and absorb disputes; incentivizes volume over fit; marketplace associations | No, not at launch |
| Pay per appointment | strongest "risk on us" story | definitions fights, no-shows, needs volume to be stable | Later, once lead flow is predictable |
| Flat monthly retainer, exclusive market | predictable, aligns with building assets the client owns, simplest contract, what the smaller exclusive-lead firms use | must prove value monthly; harder first sale | **Yes.** This is what the site now says. |
| Hybrid: setup month + retainer | funds the website build | adds a line item to explain | Yes, as the standard structure: "build month" then flat monthly |

Suggested starting structure (Lee's call, not on the site yet): a build month at a flat fee that
covers website, Google Business Profile and tracking; then a flat monthly rate by trade and market;
exclusivity written in; month to month; replacement of any lead that fails the agreed definition.
Published competitor retainers of $2,000 to $3,750 for comparable scope suggest the market will bear
a four-figure monthly number for a mid-size contractor; start below that to win proof, raise with
case studies.

## 6. Website style takeaways applied to outreachdev.dev

- Outcome headline, not a brand headline (the name "Pari" stays in the title tag and the
  wordmark only, as requested).
- The marketplace comparison block, because every strong competitor uses it and it is true.
- Three-step process, four service pillars, trades list with anchors to a trades page.
- Honest trust signals only: founder story, commitments (one per market, written price, you own it,
  month to month), the Ares Web Services track record. **No invented stats or testimonials.** As
  real results arrive, the stat strip gets numbers and the trade pages get case studies.
- A "what happens on the call" list on the contact page in place of a revenue-band qualification
  form; it lowers the barrier for a new brand.
- Pricing explained as a structure (flat, monthly, in writing) without numbers until Lee sets them.
- Full NAP in the footer and in schema; legal pages; FAQ with FAQPage schema.
